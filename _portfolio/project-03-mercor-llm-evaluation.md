## The challenge

As language models become more capable, evaluation needs to distinguish genuine reasoning from pattern matching, identify gaps in mathematical and statistical reasoning, and check how models and agents behave when they are given constraints. Research labs need problems and reviews written by domain experts, not generated by the models being tested.

## Three projects

**1. Adversarial problem sets.** Writing mathematical and statistical questions that state-of-the-art models answer wrongly but that a domain expert can solve, with a verified solution for each. Topics include probability (complex distributions, conditional probability, expectation), statistical inference (hypothesis testing, confidence intervals, causal inference), optimisation (constrained optimisation, duality) and applied problem formulation.

**2. Agentic frameworks under constraints.** Defining agentic frameworks and model behaviours under several simultaneous constraints, then evaluating how models perform when those constraints apply.

**3. STEM review.** Exploring datasets and producing data visualisations, diagrams and infographics, while reviewing the science and the data behind every output so that what is shown is correct, not just plausible.

## Impact

Contributed to evaluation frameworks used by leading AI research labs, helping to identify reasoning limitations in state-of-the-art models and to measure how they behave under constraints.

## What I learned

**Evaluating intelligence requires domain depth.** Effective benchmarks come from expertise in the subject and in where models fail.

**Human-versus-model comparison is revealing.** Problems that are hard for models but solvable by domain experts expose interesting capability gaps.

**Iterate.** Continuous testing and refinement of problem sets is what improves evaluation quality.
